Regenerative Agriculture

Regenerative agriculture is an approach to farming that puts the health of the land at the centre of production. It focuses on restoring soil health, supporting biodiversity, managing water and inputs more responsibly, and building farming systems that can remain productive over time.

Restore the Soil

Healthy soil supports healthier crops. Practices such as mulching, organic fertilisation and cover cropping help improve soil structure, retain moisture and return organic matter to the land.

Work with Nature

Regenerative farming reduces dependence on chemical inputs by making greater use of biological processes through integrated pest management and biological inputs (compost).

Beyond One Crop

Intercropping introduces additional crops into farming systems. Beyond supporting biodiversity, these practices can make productive use of available land and create additional sources of food or income.

Grow Resilience

Better soil and resource management can improve crop health, reduce costs, and build more resilient farms. For smallholders, this creates opportunities to diversify livelihoods and adapt to changing conditions.

Regeneration in Practice

Lasting change grows through continuous learning and practice.

Through Farmer Field School, LIVE PALM brings farmers together to learn, test and observe regenerative practices in the field. The approach combines practical knowledge with local experience, allowing farmers to understand what works for their land and apply it to their own farms.
